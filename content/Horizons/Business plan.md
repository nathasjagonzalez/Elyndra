# The Portugal "Empresa na Hora" Plan

Since you are a Swedish citizen living in Portugal, you have the advantage of "Freedom of Establishment" within the EU. Portugal has a "Business in an Hour" system that is surprisingly efficient for such a bureaucratic country.  

1. Get Your NIFs Ready: You likely already have one, but your husband will need one too.

2. Pick a Name & Articles: Visit an Empresa na Hora office. You can choose a name from a pre-approved list or bring a "Certificate of Admissibility" if you have a specific name in mind.

3. The "One-Hour" Visit: Go to a registry office with your IDs. You will sign the Articles of Association and receive your company's Social Security number immediately.

4. Deposit Capital: You have 5 days to deposit your share capital (e.g., €500 or €1,000) into a new business bank account.  

5. The Accountant (TOC): You must hire a Técnico Oficial de Contas. They have to sign off on your "Declaration of Beginning of Activity" within 15 days of registration.  

6. Portuguese Entity (Limitada): To operate locally in Ericeira, you’ll likely want a Portuguese Sociedade por Quotas (Lda). This allows you to hire local staff and handle Portuguese VAT (IVA).

7. EU Expansion: To work in Sweden or France, your accountant will register you in the VIES (VAT Information Exchange System). This allows you to invoice clients across the EU without charging Portuguese VAT (Reverse Charge). Consult a "Contabilista" - Find a Portuguese accountant familiar with NHR (Non-Habitual Resident) status (if you have it) or cross-border EU VAT. This will save you thousands in the expansion phase.

## The "Europe-Wide" Checklist (The Boxes to Tick)

The European Expansion: If you want to do events in Sweden, France, or Italy, you don’t necessarily need a new company in every country. Under EU law, a Portuguese company can provide "cross-border services" temporarily. If you eventually want a permanent presence elsewhere, you could look into a Societas Europaea (SE), though for a startup, a simple Portuguese HQ with a "Secondary Establishment" in Sweden is more common.

Regardless of where you register, if you want to be a "European" company, you need:

- EORI Number: Essential for moving your high-end tents across borders (e.g., if you buy them from outside the EU or ship them to the UK/Norway).
    
- Public Liability Insurance (EU-wide): Standard event insurance often only covers one country. You need a policy that covers "Europe-wide operations" for festivals and weddings.
    
- A-1 Certificates: If you hire staff in Portugal to work a festival in Sweden, you’ll need these forms to prove they are paying social security in their home country. 
    

Legal "Grey Area" Awareness: In Portugal, the PDM (Plano Diretor Municipal) is your bible. Some land allows "Turismo em Espaço Rural," while others are strictly protected. Since you want to be "mobile" (festivals/weddings), you may be able to operate under an Event License rather than a permanent Tourist License, which is often much easier to obtain.
