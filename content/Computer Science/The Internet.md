**The internet connects to the electromagnetic (EM) field by converting digital data into radio waves—a form of electromagnetic radiation—transmitted between devices and routers. Wi-Fi uses specific frequencies (2.4–5 GHz) to create oscillating electric and magnetic fields that carry information through the air, which are then received and converted back into electrical signals. 

Key Connections Between Internet and Electromagnetic Fields:

- Wireless Transmission (Wi-Fi/Cellular):Routers and devices act as antennas, creating, transmitting, and receiving electromagnetic waves to communicate data wirelessly.
    
- Radio Frequency Identification (RF): These signals operate as low-power RF-EMF (radiofrequency electromagnetic fields), which are a type of non-ionizing radiation within the broader electromagnetic spectrum.
    
- Data Conversion: Devices translate 0s and 1s into fluctuating electric and magnetic fields. The antenna in a device uses these fields to move electrons, creating alternating currents (voltage) that represent data.
    
- Fiber Optics: In cable-based internet, information is transmitted as light pulses, which are also part of the electromagnetic spectrum, moving through fiber optic cables. 
    

Wi-Fi uses frequencies similar to microwaves but at much lower power levels, typically around 2 watts, designed for short-range communication.**
