==The Greek prefix "a-" (ἀ-) or "an-" (ἀν- before vowels) means "not" or "without,"== often referred to as the **alpha privative**. It is used to indicate the absence or lack of something in many English words derived from Greek, such as "atypical" (not typical), "asymptomatic" (without symptoms), and "amorphous" (without shape). 

Key details regarding the Greek "a-" prefix:

- **Meaning:** It denotes want, absence, negation, or "not".
- **Usage:** It functions as a prefix to a root word.
- **An- Variant:** Changes to _an-_ (ἀν-) before a base starting with a vowel, as in _anemia_ (without blood) or _anarchy_ (without rule).
- **Examples:** _Atheist_ (no god), _amnesia_ (without memory), _asocial_ (not social), _anonymous_ (without a name).
- **Distinction:** It is distinct from the rare Greek _alpha copulativum_ (a- or ha-), which expresses union or togetherness. 

This prefix functions similarly to the English prefix "un-" or the Latin "in-".
