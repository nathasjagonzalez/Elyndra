#Hinduism [[Vishnu]]. Son of [[Devaki]] and [[Vasudeva]] / [[Nanda]] and [[Yashoda]] / Love of [[Radha]]

Krishna is one of the most revered deities in Hinduism, considered a Purna Avatara (a complete and perfect manifestation) of the preserver god, Vishnu. His life is seen as an expression of divine joy, strategic wisdom, and unconditional love. His very name is said to come from the Sanskrit root Karshana, meaning "the one who pulls you towards him," signifying his irresistible spiritual magnetism that draws all beings to the divine.

Krishna's life unfolds across several distinct phases, each revealing a different facet of his divine character. From a miraculous birth to his final, world-altering role as a divine teacher, his journey is a masterclass in living a life of purpose, joy, and detachment.
## **BIRTH**
The Miraculous Birth Krishna was born in a prison in the kingdom of **Mathura** to his mother, [[Devaki]], and father, [[Vasudeva]]. ==**A prophecy had foretold that Devaki's eighth child would be the cause of death for her tyrannical brother, King [[Kamsa]]**==. Terrified, Kamsa imprisoned the couple and killed their children one by one. 

Krishna was born at midnight on a stormy night. In a moment of divine intervention, the prison guards fell into a deep sleep, and the locked gates swung open. Vasudeva was divinely guided to carry the infant across the raging [[Yamuna River]] to the village of [[Gokul]]. Vasudeva carried the infant Krishna in a basket across the raging [[Yamuna River]]. Legend says the river parted to let them through, and the great serpent [[Shesha]] rose to shield the baby from the rain.

Once in [[Gokul]], he was secretly swapped with the newborn daughter of the village chief, [[Nanda]], and his wife, Yashoda. However, this was more than a simple exchange. At the same moment Vishnu manifested to Devaki and Vasudeva in the prison, a separate, original form of Krishna from his eternal abode, [[Goloka]], manifested as a beam of light to [[Nanda]] and [[Yashoda]]. The two forms then merged, making him the son of Nanda (nandasutaya) not just by adoption, but by a direct divine appearance.

As Krishna grew, the family eventually moved from Gokul to the lush forests of **Vrindavan** to escape the demons Kansa kept sending. This is where the atmosphere shifts from survival to "Madhurya" (sweetness). Krishna became the "Gopala" (protector of cows) and the "Makhan Chor" (butter thief), enchanting everyone with his flute.
## LIFE 
The Mischievous Childhood Growing up in the pastoral village of [[Gokul]], Krishna was a joyful and irrepressible child. His love for butter earned him the endearing name "Makan Chore" (the butter thief), and his childhood was filled with playful pranks and miracles that hinted at his true nature. In one famous incident, he effortlessly lifted the massive Govardhan Hill with his little finger to shield the villagers from a torrential storm. On another occasion, when his mother [[Yashoda]] scolded him for eating mud and forced his mouth open, she witnessed a reality-shattering vision. It was not just the universe she saw, but universe within universe, with parallel realities playing out simultaneously. In a moment of cosmic recursion akin to inception, she saw herself in a parallel universe scolding another Krishna, who opened his mouth to reveal yet another universe within.

The Divine Lover of Vrindavan As a youth in the nearby village of Vrindavan, Krishna's divine charm captivated everyone. The music from his flute was so enchanting that it would cause birds to stop mid-flight and the river to pause its flow. He was especially beloved by the [[Gopis]] (the cow-herd maidens), whose relationship with him symbolized the soul's intense love for the divine. His eternal companion during this time was [[Radha]], whose devotion was so complete that their names are almost always spoken as one. The pinnacle of this era was the [[Rasa Lila]], or the divine circle dance, where he miraculously multiplied himself to dance with every gopi simultaneously, showing that divine love is both infinite and deeply personal.

The King and Strategist Leaving the idyllic life of Vrindavan behind, Krishna returned to [[Mathura]] to fulfill his destiny. He confronted and killed his tyrannical uncle [[Kamsa]], freeing the kingdom from his oppressive rule. Later, he founded the magnificent golden city of [[Dwaraka]] on the coast of modern-day Gujarat, establishing a prosperous and just kingdom. In this phase, Krishna revealed himself as a master statesman and an expert strategist, guiding other rulers and becoming the political and spiritual center of his time.

The Charioteer of Destiny Krishna's most pivotal role came during the epic [[Mahabharata]] war, a colossal conflict between two sets of cousins: the noble Pandavas and the arrogant Kauravas. Choosing not to fight, Krishna served as the unarmed charioteer for the Pandava prince, Arjuna. On the battlefield of Kurukshetra, as Arjuna faltered at the thought of fighting his own kin, Krishna delivered the timeless wisdom of the [[Bhagavad Gita]] ("The Song of the Lord"). This sacred dialogue on duty, action, and the nature of reality remains one of the world's most profound spiritual texts.

### **The "First" Meeting with Radha**

The meeting between Radha and Krishna is often described differently depending on the tradition, but two versions are particularly famous:

1. **The Childhood Recognition:** In some accounts, they met as very young children when Nanda (Krishna's father) and Vrishabhanu (Radha's father) met. Even as toddlers, it is said there was an immediate, transcendental recognition of their eternal bond.
    
2. **The Forest Encounter:** The more popular poetic version happens when they are slightly older. Krishna is playing his flute in the forest or by the Yamuna, and Radha, hearing the music, is drawn to him. It’s often described as a "moment out of time"—where the ordinary world falls away and they realize they are two halves of the same soul.
    

> **Note on the Gopis:** The Gopis (the cowherd girls of Vrindavan) represent the height of devotion. While they all loved Krishna, Radha is considered the "Maha-bhava," the personification of the highest form of that love.
